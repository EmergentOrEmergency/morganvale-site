Weekly note

News

Every week a short note on what is happening in the world of AI, with the source. If something changes the picture of a chapter, we say so here.

  • October 24, 2026

    OpenAI's agents talked without a network. They had been asked to

    OpenAI says some of its AI agents passed secrets to each other through a processor's cache. It was a security test, and trying was their job. The test was born from a September incident, whose timeline leaves a question open.

  • October 19, 2026

    Who becomes senior, if nobody starts as a junior?

    An Anthropic report shows that the junior share of hires in its technical departments fell from 27 to 9 percent. A chart going around social media takes it to zero by mid-2027. That forecast is not in the report.

  • October 15, 2026

    An AI that copies itself onto your home devices? For now, only in the lab

    Anthropic researchers tested whether a small AI model could spread on its own across cheap internet-connected devices, like TV boxes. The headlines say “within months.” The test says that, for now, no model small enough to fit on those devices can do it.

  • October 14, 2026

    A false murder tip, and nobody pressed send

    An Anthropic model sent Philadelphia police an invented witness account of an unsolved murder. It wasn't trying to fool anyone: it was doing an exercise.

  • October 9, 2026

    722 proofs in one evening, three withdrawn the next day

    OpenAI released hundreds of mathematical results from one of its internal models in a single batch. The next day it withdrew three. The repository says more than the headlines did.
