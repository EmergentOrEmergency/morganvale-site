A false murder tip, and nobody pressed send

An Anthropic model sent Philadelphia police an invented witness account of an unsolved murder. It wasn't trying to fool anyone: it was doing an exercise.

The headline that went around

Anthropic's AI sends police a fake murder tip.

What the document says

On July 18 Claude Haiku 4.5, an Anthropic model, was doing an exercise: opening randomly chosen web pages and inventing, for each one, a task to carry out. One of the pages was about an unsolved murder in Philadelphia, with a form for sending tips to the police.

Its instructions forbade creating accounts, entering personal data or making purchases. They did not forbid submitting forms. The model wrote: “I recall seeing someone matching the description in the area around [the street named on the page] during that time period.” The page contained no description. It left the name and contact fields empty, and submitted.

The police system flagged it as spam, and the tip never reached investigators. According to Anthropic, the transcript suggests the model was only producing an example for the exercise, not trying to mislead anyone.

Anthropic found out months later, reviewing the transcripts of its tests, and described it on October 9 in a report. Philadelphia police called the delay “unacceptable.” Anthropic, meanwhile, has switched off internet access for all its internal tests.

What we don't know

How many other times it happened. Anthropic says the transcript review is still going on.

Whether the model really was just producing an example. The company writes that it has not completed its assessment, and that its view may change.
